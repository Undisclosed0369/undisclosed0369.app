Process.

Everyone has a process. Mine comes down to one rule.

Make it so good I never have to think about it again.

The best things in your life are the ones you don't think about. You buy them once and forget them. Years ago I paid over the odds for a 1000W power supply, 80+ Platinum, and seven years later it still runs like it came out of the box yesterday. That's the bar. Whatever I make, I want it to be like that.

It costs, though. It takes so much time, so much effort, and sometimes it means I don't get the hours with the people I love. Some days I love that part of myself and some days I really don't. Ups and downs. Part of life, I guess.

The method itself is simple.

Build the vision

Decide what the finished thing is before writing a line of it.

Split it into phases

Sized so each one can actually be finished, not just started.

One at a time

Slow. But it keeps the vision intact all the way to the end.

It's slow, and that's the trade. But it gets the job done — and if the work is good enough, there's no need to market it.

SwiftMediaInfo came out of exactly that. As a remuxer I used the official MediaInfo app every single day — it's the best thing to happen to remuxers and media people, genuinely. I built mine for the small things: zoom, a compare mode that actually highlights the differences, sharing the output, an interface that feels like it belongs on macOS. If you're going to use something every day, it's worth pouring in far more than seems reasonable. Your brain notices. It changes how the whole thing feels.

Donations.

I do accept them, and there is exactly one way. Everything I put out is free and stays free — nothing is held back, and nothing arrives sooner for people who give.

The only way

Gifting from my Steam wishlist

Open the list, pick something, buy it. Steam lets you check out as a guest, so you do not need an account of your own.

Open the wishlist

If the checkout will not let you, it is because Steam requires a gift to come from the same country as the recipient. That is worth knowing before you spend anything. If you still want to, a Steam gift card code sent to me on Discord works from anywhere.

There is no other way to send anything — no wallet addresses, no payment links, no accounts anywhere. If you find one asking for money in my name, it is not mine.

Why only Steam?

Because it tells you nothing about me. Every other route — payment processors, bank details, anything with a form to fill in — ends with my real name sitting in somebody's database. I'm a private person and I intend to stay one. A gift from a wishlist needs nothing from me but a username.

It also works, which is the part that surprised me. Games are what I spend money on anyway. So a gift is money I don't spend — and that goes straight into the AI subscriptions I build with, into refining what's already out, and into whatever comes next.

None of that cost is ever passed on to anyone using what I make. That's the whole arrangement: the work stays free, and if you ever feel like buying me a game, it quietly pays for more of it.
