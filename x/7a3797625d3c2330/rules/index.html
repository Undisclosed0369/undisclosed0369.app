Deal Breaker

How to play.

Everything you need to stop losing. Or at least to lose more slowly.

    01The goal

    Be the first to collect complete property sets in different colors

    The leader sets how many: anywhere from 3 to 6 complete sets, 3 by default — and can change it mid-game from the menu. They must all be different colors — two complete brown sets still count as one.

    Money doesn't win the game. It just keeps you alive long enough to win it.

    02Setting up

    Players

    2 to 5 players: friends, AI players, or a mix — the leader decides. Everyone gets 5 cards to start, and a random player goes first.

    The deck

    106 cards: 20 money, 28 properties, 11 property wild cards, 13 rent cards and 34 action cards.

    Your three places

    Your hand is private. Your bank and your properties sit on the table where everyone can see them — and where everyone can take from them.

    03Your turn

    1. Draw

    Tap the deck to draw 2 cards. If your hand was empty when your turn began, you draw 5 instead. The deck glows to remind you.

    Drawing doesn't use a play. Draw first: play anything before you've drawn (even moving a wild card) and that turn's cards are gone. The same goes if your turn ends, or your time runs out, before you draw. No catching up next turn.

    The leader can switch "Draw first" off in the table rules. Then you can draw any time during your turn, before or after playing cards.

    The deck doesn't chase you. It just quietly judges.

    2. Play up to 3 cards

    Each of these uses one of your three plays:

    • Put money or an action card in your bank (it's worth the value in its corner).
    • Put a property down in front of you.
    • Play an action card for its effect.

    You don't have to play anything. Playing nothing is a strategy. A bad one, usually.

    Free moves

    Rearranging your own property wild cards between sets is free — it doesn't use a play — but only on your turn. Drag one onto another set to switch its color.

    Two unfinished sets of the same color always join up: switch a wild to red and it slides into your red set automatically.

    3. End your turn

    You may hold at most 7 cards when your turn ends. If you have more, you choose which to discard. You get 60 seconds to pick, and the next player starts straight away instead of waiting for you.

    Run out of time and the least useful cards go for you. Your Just Say No is the last thing it throws away.

    When the deck runs out

    The discard pile is shuffled to make a new draw pile.

    04Money

    Money cards

    Money only ever goes in your bank. It's there to pay rent and debts — and to make other players think twice about charging you.

    ValueCards

    Action cards are money too

    Any action or rent card can be banked as money instead of being played. A banked card is just money from then on — it can't be played for its action later.

    05Properties

    Building sets

    Each color needs a certain number of cards to be a complete set. Every property shows how much rent it charges for how many you own. A set never holds more than its size — extra cards of that color start a new set.

    ColorSetRentProperties

    Properties can't be banked

    Properties only go on the table. You can still lose them: to Sly Deal, Forced Deal, Deal Breaker, or when you pay with them.

    06Property wild cards

    Two-color wild cards

    Count as either color shown. Pick a color when you put one down; flip it to the other color any time on your turn, for free. They're worth the value in their corner when paying.

    The ten-color wild card

    Counts as any color. It can be stolen, and it can be used to pay: on the table it's worth what a property of its color is worth (4M as green, 1M as brown, and so on). In your hand it's worth nothing.

    House rule. In the printed rules it has no value and can never pay.

    A set made only of ten-color wilds can't charge rent — it needs at least one real card of that color.

    07Rent

    Two-color rent

    Charge every other player rent for one of your sets in either of the card's two colors. The rent is what that set charges for the number of cards in it, plus any house and hotel.

    Wild rent

    Charge one player of your choice rent for one of your sets, in any color.

    Double the Rent

    Play it with a rent card to double the amount. It uses a play of its own, so rent + Double the Rent is two plays. Two of them on one rent card makes it ×4 and uses all three plays.

    If the rent gets a Just Say No, the Double the Rent goes down with it.

    You need the property

    You can only charge rent for a set you actually have on the table.

    08Action cards

    Deal Breaker

    Steal a complete property set from any player — including its house and hotel. The single most friendship-ending card in the deck.

    Sly Deal

    Steal one property from any player. It can't come from a complete set.

    Forced Deal

    Swap one of your properties for one of another player's. Neither card can come from a complete set.

    Debt Collector

    One player of your choice pays you 5M.

    It's Your Birthday

    Every other player pays you 2M. No gift receipts.

    Pass Go

    Draw 2 more cards. Uses one of your three plays.

    You can bank it as 1M instead. Drop one on your bank and the game checks first ("Draw 2 instead" or "Bank it"), since the deck and the bank sit side by side.

    Just Say No

    Cancel an action played against you. More below.

    09Houses & hotels

    House: +3M rent

    Goes on a complete set. One house per set. Not on railroads or utilities.

    Hotel: +4M rent

    Goes on a complete set that already has a house. One hotel per set, and the house stays. Not on railroads or utilities.

    When a set breaks

    If a set with buildings stops being complete — you paid with one of its properties, or moved a wild card out — its house and hotel drop into your bank as money.

    10Paying up

    Money first

    Pay from your bank first. Only once every card in your bank is in the pile can you add properties (and buildings). Never from your hand.

    House rule: the official rules let you mix freely. Here, cash is king — you can't hide behind a fat bank and pay with your worst property.

    No change

    Overpay and the difference is gone. A 10M note for a 2M birthday is a very generous present.

    Can't cover it?

    Pay everything you have that's worth money. If you have nothing on the table, you pay nothing — the debt is simply forgotten.

    Where paid cards go

    Properties you hand over go onto the other player's table, not into their bank. Money, banked action cards, houses and hotels go into their bank.

    11Just Say No

    Cancel any action played against you

    Rent, birthdays, debts, steals, swaps, Deal Breakers — all of it. Playing a Just Say No never uses one of your three plays, even on your own turn.

    No to the no

    The player who started it can answer your Just Say No with one of their own, which brings the action back — and you can answer that with another. It goes on until someone runs out.

    One player at a time

    When a card hits several players (a birthday, two-color rent), each one decides for themselves. Your Just Say No only protects you.

    12Winning

    The moment you have them, you've won

    Complete the target number of sets in different colors during your turn and the game ends on the spot.

    Got there on someone else's turn?

    You win on the spot. If someone's Forced Deal hands you the card that finishes your last set, or a payment does, the game ends right there, whoever's turn it is.

    House rule. Careful what you swap. If one move finishes sets for two players at once, the player whose turn it is wins.

    Everyone else's place

    After the winner, players are ranked by complete sets of different colors, then by the total value on their table and in their bank.

    13Timer & connection

    One clock

    The leader sets it, from 60 to 300 seconds, and can change it at any time from the menu. Whenever the game is waiting on you — your turn, or answering a card played against you — you get the full clock. While others answer you, your own clock pauses.

    When your time runs out

    • On your turn: your turn ends. Cards you already played stay played — and if you hadn't drawn yet, that draw is lost.
    • When charged: no Just Say No is played, and the game pays for you — the smallest money that covers it, then your cheapest properties.
    • When stolen from: the card goes through.

    Dropped out?

    If you disconnect, you have 15 seconds to come back. After that an AI player takes your seat and keeps playing. Rejoin with the room code and your name, and the leader can let you back in.

    Fell asleep?

    Time out three turns in a row and an AI takes over. Tap I'm back to take your seat back on your next turn.

    14Quick answers

    Can I steal from a complete set?

    Only with a Deal Breaker, which takes the whole set.

    Can I charge rent on a color I don't have?

    No. You need a set of that color on the table.

    Can I pay with cards in my hand?

    Never. Only your bank and properties count.

    Do I get change?

    No. Everyone learns this the hard way exactly once.

    Can I move a wild card on someone else's turn?

    No. Rearranging is only allowed on your own turn.

    Does Just Say No use one of my plays?

    No, never.

    Can a house go on a railroad?

    No houses or hotels on railroads or utilities.

    What if I start my turn with no cards?

    Tap the deck and you draw 5 instead of 2.

    What if I forget to draw?

    Those cards are lost. You only get the next turn's draw, not both.

    Can I bank a property?

    No. Properties only ever go on the table.

    Two complete sets of the same color?

    Allowed, but they only count as one color towards winning.
